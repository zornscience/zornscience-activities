# AP Chemistry Unit 5 Practice checks

Version: 1.0.3

## Answer audit, worked before authoring the question bank

Original practice aligned to College Board AP Chemistry CED Unit 5, Kinetics, topics 5.1 to 5.11. These are not official exam items. The solutions below were established before the question bank. Concentration-based laws assume constant temperature and volume. Energy diagrams are schematic models, not measured data. Numerical energy levels are in kJ/mol. Elementary-step and pre-equilibrium mechanisms are proposed models; agreement with a rate law supports but does not prove a mechanism.

5.1-1 | A reactant concentration decreases. Its positive disappearance rate is −Δ[A]/Δt. | Negative secant slope with sign reversed
5.1-2 | A tangent gives the local derivative; a secant averages over an interval. | Tangent slope at that instant, sign reversed for disappearance
5.1-3 | 2NO<sub>2</sub>(g) → 2NO(g) + O<sub>2</sub>(g). Divide disappearance of NO<sub>2</sub> by 2 to get formation of O<sub>2</sub>. | Oxygen forms half as fast as nitrogen dioxide disappears
5.1-4 | Typical closed-batch reactant concentrations fall; fewer collisions reduce velocity for a positive-order reactant. | Rate usually decreases as reactant is consumed
5.1-5 | (0.800 − 0.500) M / 15.0 s. | 0.0200 M/s
5.1-6 | N<sub>2</sub>(g) + 3H<sub>2</sub>(g) → 2NH<sub>3</sub>(g). Hydrogen disappears at (3/2)(0.0400). | 0.0600 M/s
5.2-1 | Initial rates: [A], [B], rate = (0.10,0.10,0.0020), (0.20,0.10,0.0080), (0.10,0.20,0.0040). Doubling A quadruples rate; doubling B doubles rate. | rate = k[A]<sup>2</sup>[B]
5.2-2 | Exponents 2 and 1 sum, not multiply. | Overall order 3
5.2-3 | Rate has units M/s; divide by M cubed. | k units M<sup>−2</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>
5.2-4 | An overall reaction is not necessarily elementary; orders come from measurements. | Coefficients alone do not establish the rate law
5.2-5 | k = 0.00800 / ((0.200)<sup>2</sup>(0.100)). | 2.00 M<sup>−2</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>
5.2-6 | At fixed B and temperature, a zero-order dependence on A gives (3)<sup>0</sup> = 1. | Rate ratio 1, exact
5.3-1 | Zero-order integrated law [A] = [A]<sub>0</sub> − kt. Model line falls from 0.800 to 0.400 M in 40.0 s. | [A] versus time is linear
5.3-2 | First-order integrated law ln[A] = ln[A]<sub>0</sub> − kt. | ln[A] versus time is linear
5.3-3 | Second-order integrated law 1/[A] = 1/[A]<sub>0</sub> + kt. | 1/[A] versus time is linear
5.3-4 | For first order, each half-life reduces the remaining concentration by 1/2, independent of its starting value. | First-order half-life is concentration independent
5.3-5 | First order, k = 0.0231 s<sup>−1</sup>: t½ = 0.693/0.0231. | 30.0 s
5.3-6 | Second order: 1/[A] = 1/0.500 + (0.200)(10.0) = 4.00 M<sup>−1</sup>. | 0.250 M
5.4-1 | One reacting molecule in A(g) → B(g) + C(g) is unimolecular. | Unimolecular
5.4-2 | Elementary 2NO(g) → N<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>(g) has two colliding molecules. | Bimolecular with rate = k[NO]<sup>2</sup>
5.4-3 | Elementary A(g) + B(g) → C(g) uses one of each reactant. | rate = k[A][B]
5.4-4 | Three bodies simultaneously having appropriate energy and orientation is less probable than two. | Termolecular events are less likely
5.4-5 | Elementary rate = k[A][B], k = 0.500 M<sup>−1</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, A = 0.200 M, B = 0.300 M. | 0.0300 M/s
5.4-6 | Elementary A + 2B requires one A and two B reacting particles. | Molecularity 3, exact
5.5-1 | Collision theory requires both sufficient energy and appropriate orientation. | Both conditions are necessary for a successful collision
5.5-2 | Increasing temperature broadens the distribution and increases the fraction above a fixed activation threshold. | Larger energetic fraction
5.5-3 | At fixed temperature, increasing gas concentration increases collisions per unit volume and time; energy distribution is unchanged in this model. | Collision frequency rises
5.5-4 | Arrhenius k = A exp(−E<sub>a</sub>/RT); with A and E<sub>a</sub> fixed and E<sub>a</sub> positive, raising T increases k. | k increases
5.5-5 | Count model: 200 collisions × 0.30 sufficient energy × 0.20 favorable orientation among energetic collisions. | Expected 12 successful collisions, exact model inputs
5.5-6 | At the same T and A, lower E<sub>a</sub> raises k. Given k(new)/k(old) = 4.00 and k(old) = 0.0250 s<sup>−1</sup>. | 0.100 s<sup>−1</sup>
5.6-1 | Single-step profile R = 20, peak = 80, P = 0. The peak is an unstable activated complex, not an isolable intermediate. | Peak is transition state
5.6-2 | ΔH = products − reactants = 0 − 20. | Exothermic, ΔH = −20 kJ/mol
5.6-3 | Reverse activation barrier = 80 − 0, larger than forward 80 − 20. | Reverse barrier is 80 kJ/mol
5.6-4 | Horizontal position tracks progress of bond rearrangement, not elapsed time. | Reaction coordinate
5.6-5 | Forward activation barrier = 80 − 20. | 60.0 kJ/mol, requested three significant figures
5.6-6 | Separate endothermic model R = 10.0, P = 35.0. ΔH = 35.0 − 10.0. | +25.0 kJ/mol
5.7-1 | NO<sub>2</sub> + NO<sub>2</sub> → NO<sub>3</sub> + NO; NO<sub>3</sub> + CO → NO<sub>2</sub> + CO<sub>2</sub>. Add and cancel NO<sub>3</sub> and one NO<sub>2</sub>. | NO<sub>2</sub>(g) + CO(g) → NO(g) + CO<sub>2</sub>(g)
5.7-2 | NO<sub>3</sub> is made in step 1 and consumed in step 2; absent from net equation. | NO<sub>3</sub> is an intermediate
5.7-3 | Cl is consumed in Cl + O<sub>3</sub> → ClO + O<sub>2</sub>, then regenerated in ClO + O → Cl + O<sub>2</sub>. | Cl is a catalyst
5.7-4 | Mechanisms must sum to the overall reaction and agree with experimental kinetics. | Both requirements are necessary
5.7-5 | One intermediate species, NO<sub>3</sub>, in the stated two-step NO<sub>2</sub>/CO model. | 1 intermediate species, exact
5.7-6 | Summing chlorine steps gives O<sub>3</sub> + O → 2O<sub>2</sub>. | Oxygen coefficient 2, exact
5.8-1 | Slow first elementary step NO<sub>2</sub> + NO<sub>2</sub> → NO<sub>3</sub> + NO controls rate; fast second consumes NO<sub>3</sub> with CO. | rate = k[NO<sub>2</sub>]<sup>2</sup>
5.8-2 | CO occurs only in the subsequent fast step of the stated slow-first mechanism. | Predicted zero order in CO under these conditions
5.8-3 | Slow A + B → I, fast I + B → P. Overall A + 2B → P but slow law is k[A][B]. | Overall B coefficient does not set its order
5.8-4 | Observed rate = k[A]<sup>2</sup> contradicts a proposed slow first elementary A + B → I law k[A][B]. | Proposed mechanism is inconsistent
5.8-5 | Slow-first bimolecular model k = 0.400 M<sup>−1</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, A = 0.250 M, B = 0.200 M. | 0.0200 M/s
5.8-6 | Slow elementary A + B law, both concentrations doubled: 2 × 2. | Rate factor 4, exact
5.9-1 | Fast A + B ⇌ I implies k<sub>1</sub>[A][B] = k<sub>−1</sub>[I]. | [I] = (k<sub>1</sub>/k<sub>−1</sub>)[A][B]
5.9-2 | Slow I + B → P has rate k<sub>2</sub>[I][B]; substitute pre-equilibrium I. | rate = k<sub>2</sub>(k<sub>1</sub>/k<sub>−1</sub>)[A][B]<sup>2</sup>
5.9-3 | Equilibration of the reversible first step is much faster than I consumption by the slow step. | Fast pre-equilibrium assumption
5.9-4 | In the same mechanism, doubling B at fixed A and temperature squares its factor. | Rate quadruples
5.9-5 | [I] = K<sub>c</sub>[A][B], K<sub>c</sub> = 4.00 M<sup>−1</sup>, A = 0.100 M, B = 0.200 M (free equilibrium concentrations). | 0.0800 M
5.9-6 | k<sub>2</sub> = 0.500 M<sup>−1</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, I = 0.0800 M, B = 0.200 M. | 0.00800 M/s
5.10-1 | Two peaks in R20, TS1 70, I40, TS2 100, P10 model. | Two elementary steps
5.10-2 | One valley between peaks represents a species with finite lifetime. | One intermediate
5.10-3 | Local barriers: 70 − 20 = 50; 100 − 40 = 60. Under stated comparable prefactors, step 2 is slower. | Step 2, based on its local barrier
5.10-4 | Maxima represent transition states, minima between them represent intermediates. | Two transition states and one intermediate
5.10-5 | Second-step forward activation energy = 100 − 40. | 60.0 kJ/mol
5.10-6 | Overall ΔH = 10 − 20; intermediate and peaks cancel. | −10.0 kJ/mol
5.11-1 | Catalyst provides another pathway with a lower activation barrier. | Alternative pathway
5.11-2 | Model R20, P0 for both paths, uncatalyzed peak90, catalyzed peak55. Endpoints unchanged. | Same ΔH, lower barrier
5.11-3 | Catalyst speeds forward and reverse reactions, reaches equilibrium sooner, does not change equilibrium composition at fixed temperature. | Equilibrium constant unchanged
5.11-4 | Solid catalyst with gaseous reactants offers surface sites; more exposed sites can increase rate. | Heterogeneous catalysis
