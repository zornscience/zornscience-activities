The Molar Mass Trap Lab

Meet the four liquids

Water is the lightest molecule in this lab at 18 g/mol. Mineral oil molecules are more than 15 times heavier. If molar mass were all that mattered, water would lose every test. Find out where water wins, where it loses, and which intermolecular forces explain each result.

Test 1 checks how each liquid beads or spreads on wax paper. Test 2 times a steel shot falling through each liquid. Test 3 mixes pairs of liquids to see if they form layers.

About this simulation

Each sample uses about 0.15 mL on the same wax paper. The puddle dimensions are simulated. Wetting depends on cohesion within the liquid and adhesion to the wax. A diameter describes spreading on this surface.

The shot times are simulated for one set of sealed tubes at room temperature. The same shot and travel distance are used in each tube. Density and drag also affect the fall time.

The mixing rack shows approximate layer volumes for any two liquids. The pipette adds 0.5 mL of 1-octanol, as on the lab worksheet, and 1.0 mL of the other liquids. The dyes help you see where the liquids went. Two visible layers can still contain a small amount of each other. Glycerin and 1-octanol are not paired in the classroom lab. The simulation shows them as two layers because they barely dissolve in each other.

The clocks run faster than real time. The 30 s wax-paper settling takes about 2.5 s, and the 60 s mixing stand takes 10 s.

Wax-paper investigation

  1. Compare the four liquids. Look at each Lewis structure and molar mass.
  2. Make your predictions on your lab worksheet.
  3. Choose Start the lab to open the three tests. Use Intro to come back to this page.
  1. Choose W, G, O or M. Add three drops into one spot in its box.
  2. Watch the puddle settle until the stopwatch reads 0:30. Look from above and at eye level.
  3. Move the ruler so its zero mark lines up with the left edge. Read the right edge in mm. Use Read edges to hear or see both readings.
  4. Read the height on the upright ruler in the eye-level view. The dashed line marks the top of the puddle.
  5. Record the diameter and sketch the side view on your lab worksheet. Repeat for all four liquids.
  1. Choose a sealed tube. Tilt it to move the shot to the stopper.
  2. Turn the tube upright. The stopwatch starts when the shot leaves the stopper and stops when it first touches the bottom.
  3. Record two trials on your lab worksheet and find the average in seconds. A fall faster than 0.5 s reads less than 0.5 s.
  4. Tilt the tube again to return the shot to the stopper for another trial.
  1. Choose any numbered tube. Use the pipette to add two liquids. It adds 1.0 mL of each liquid, except 0.5 mL of 1-octanol. Your lab worksheet lists the pairs to test.
  2. Add a stopper. Gently invert five times. The tube goes back upright on its own and the stopwatch starts.
  3. Let it stand until the stopwatch reads 1:00, or advance time. Count the liquid layers below the curved surface. Use Read layers to hear or see the bands.
  4. Record the number of layers and where the dye went. Pour the mixture into the waste beaker and repeat with the other tubes.
