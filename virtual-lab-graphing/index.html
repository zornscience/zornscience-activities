Instructions

Turn your lab measurements into a scatter plot and download a graph for your write-up.

  1. Label your graph. Add a title, the independent variable on x, the dependent variable on y, and their units. Remember DRY MIX.
  2. Enter measurements. Type one x/y pair per row, add rows as needed, or paste/import a two-column CSV. You can also load a lesson example.
  3. Look for the pattern. Turn on Linear best fit when the points follow an approximately straight trend. Read the slope, y-intercept and R² below the graph. A straight line does not describe every relationship.
  4. Read the line. Enter an x value to estimate y from the fit. Estimates outside your measured range are extrapolations.
  5. Save your work. Download PNG or SVG for your lab report, CSV to keep the data, or print the graph.

Your data stays in this tab. Closing or reloading clears it. Download CSV before leaving. Sharing sends a fresh tool link; your measurements are not included.

For your lab write-up

Read your best-fit line

y = mx + b

A best-fit line summarizes an approximately straight trend. It does not join the dots.

Slope, m
y-intercept, b
R²

R² describes how closely the points follow this straight line. Closer to 1 means a closer fit. Check the shape of your data: a high R² does not prove causation or guarantee a linear relationship.

Before you turn it in

Give your graph a descriptive title, label both variables with units, and describe what happens to y as x increases. Use a straight best-fit line only when the pattern is approximately linear.

A little graphing help

Remember DRY MIX

Dependent, Responding, Y-axis. Manipulated, Independent, X-axis. Put what you change on x and what you measure on y.

Read the scale

Each axis uses evenly spaced steps. The axes can use different step sizes. Including zero shows the origin; turning it off zooms in on the measured range.

Explain the slope

The slope is the change in y for one unit of x. Its units are y-units per x-unit. A negative slope means y tends to decrease as x increases.

Take it with you

PNG works in Google Docs and most lab reports. SVG stays sharp at any size. CSV saves your table for later. Downloads include only valid, complete data pairs.
