How to use this lab

Choose an experiment, set the initial conditions, and measure how energy moves through an insulated calorimeter.

Measure a temperature change and apply q = mcΔT.

  1. Choose an experiment. Drop a heated metal into water, or mix strong HCl and NaOH solutions.
  2. Set the conditions. For metals, choose the material, mass and temperatures. For neutralization, choose each solution’s volume and concentration.
  3. Start and observe. Follow the thermometer and temperature trace until thermal equilibrium is reached at 40 simulated seconds.
  4. Calculate and compare. Use q = mcΔT and the energy balance to find specific heat or molar enthalpy. Change a setting to reset, then compare a new run.
Try unequal reactant amounts

Mix 50 mL of 1 M HCl with 100 mL of 1 M NaOH. The acid limits water formation to 0.050 mol; compare the temperature rise with equal volumes.

Investigate & understand the model

For metals: qwater = −qmetal, so cmetal = mwater × 4.184 × (Tf − Twater) / [mmetal × (Tmetal − Tf)]. Double the metal mass and compare ΔT.

For neutralization: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O. qsolution = m × 4.184 × ΔT; ΔH = −qsolution / mol H₂O formed. Try unequal reactant amounts to find the limiting reagent.

Ideal insulated calorimeter with negligible cup heat capacity, no evaporation, perfect mixing, and solution density 1.00 g/mL and specific heat 4.184 J/(g·°C). Neutralization uses −57.3 kJ/mol. Heat transfer is accelerated; the trace is illustrative.
