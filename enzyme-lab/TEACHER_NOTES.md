# Teacher notes

## Goals

Students can explain that an enzyme is a protein whose active site fits a specific substrate; design a fair test that
changes one variable and controls the rest; read a rate graph and identify an optimum; and explain why extreme heat or
pH denatures an enzyme while cold only slows it.

## Timing

About 40 to 50 minutes for one variable. Fast groups can run the second variable; Copy my data then includes both.

## Notes

- Foam height after 60 s stands in for reaction rate. It is an indirect measure, like the floating disks in the
  Leaf Disk Lab.
- The 55 °C tube still makes a little foam: some catalase molecules have denatured and some have not.
- 80 °C and pH 3 or 11 make almost no foam. Cooling or neutralizing does not restore activity in the model,
  matching the boiled liver question.
- The hidden optimum shifts by a few degrees each session, but with the five tested levels the measured optimum is
  always 37 °C or pH 7. Students who ask "is 37 exactly the best?" have a good follow-up: test between 30 and 45 °C.
- Nothing is saved. Remind students to use Copy my data before closing the tab. Reloading clears the work.
- Real catalase from liver is very active; real results vary with freshness, the extract and the peroxide strength.
  This is an illustrative teaching model.
