# Periodic Trends Practice
Version: 1.1.1

## Assumptions and departures
- Runs on the shared activity engine (`shared/zsa-engine.js`, `shared/zsa-activity.css`) exactly as it is, like the Ion Charge Trainer and the Ionic Formula Builder: tap to check, answer trail, two hints, Show me, review queue, round summary, Practice my misses and the always-visible main-group table. `trends.js` holds the steps, the misconception rules and the question strip.
- Every question comes from the Periodic Table's data. `data.js` is generated by `tools/build-data.js`, which reads `../periodic-table/data.js`, lists every pair, checks the data against the trend rule, leaves out near ties, and writes the questions, the excluded pairs and every word a student sees. Rerun `node periodic-trends/tools/build-data.js` after changing the Periodic Table's data or the wording; do not edit `data.js` by hand.
- A question's step 3 answer is the element with the larger value in the data. A pair is asked only when that agrees with the trend rule and the two values differ by more than 2% of the larger one, so the data and the rule always give the same answer and no question is a near tie.
- Embed mode hides the back link line and the footer; the page title stays visible.
- Built and browser-tested in Chromium by Claude. Real screen readers, Firefox, Safari and school devices remain untested. See TESTING.md.

## Purpose and learning goal
Students compare two main-group elements and reason from the trend: same group or same period, which way the trend goes there, then which element has the larger atomic radius, the higher electronegativity or the higher first ionization energy. The explanations use the lessons' words: effective nuclear charge across a period, more energy levels down a group.

Lesson: https://zornscience.org/science/chemistry/unit-2-quantum/periodic-trends-periods/

Steps (shown: sodium and chlorine, atomic radius)
1. Are sodium and chlorine in the same group or the same period? Same group, Same period, Neither.
2. Across a period (left to right), how does atomic radius change? Increases, Decreases, No change. (For a group pair: "Down a group (top to bottom)".)
3. Which has the larger atomic radius? Sodium (Na), Chlorine (Cl).

Each step gives targeted feedback, two hints (general, then specific), and Show me after two incorrect attempts (steps 1 and 2; step 3 has two choices, so the second pick is always right). The summary shows both values and links to the Periodic Table in the matching heatmap with the answer selected.

## Misconception map and precedence
A correct answer is always checked first. Wording lives in `data.js` under `ui.feedback`. Every wrong pick fires exactly one rule.

| Step | Wrong pick | Rule | Targeted response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The other of group or period | relation | Both elements' group and period, then "A group is a column; a period is a row." |
| 1 | Neither | neither | "They do share a row or a column. Find both on the table: a group is a column, a period is a row." |
| 2 | No change | stays the same | "It does change." with what changes across a period (a proton added to the same energy level) or down a group (an energy level added). |
| 2 | Opposite direction, atomic radius | radius confused with ionization energy | "That is the ionization energy trend. Atomic radius goes the opposite way." plus a pointer (effective nuclear charge, or energy levels). |
| 2 | Opposite direction, ionization energy | ionization energy confused with radius | "That is the atomic radius trend. Ionization energy goes the opposite way." plus the pointer. |
| 2 | Opposite direction, electronegativity | direction reversed | "That is backwards." plus the pointer. |
| 3 | The element with the larger atomic number, when it is wrong | atomic number alone | "A larger atomic number does not always mean a larger atomic radius (or a higher ...). Use the trend: ..." |
| 3 | The element with the smaller atomic number, when it is wrong | trend applied backwards | "Use the direction you found: ... so the element farther right (or left, up, down) has the higher value." |

Hints: step 1, "Find both elements on the table..." then both elements' group and period. Step 2, what changes across a period or down a group, then the full trend sentence. Step 3, "Use the direction from step 2...", then the trend and where the first element sits. Every hint highlights the elements' group columns in the table.

## Rounds and options
- A round has 9 different questions: 3 per trend, and each trend has at least one same-group and one same-period pair. Review can add up to 3 items with the engine's spacing rules.
- First-try counts are kept per step: "Same group or period", "Trend direction", "Which element".
- Practice my misses: every missed question, then others with the same trend and relation, up to 6.
- Practice options: start a new round, or practice one trend (6 questions).
- Links from a lesson: `?trend=radius`, `?trend=en` or `?trend=ie` start a round of one trend; `?q=<question id>` (such as `?q=radius-Na-Cl`) starts one question. Question ids are listed in `data.js`.
- "Back to the lesson" goes to the Periodic Trends (Periods) lesson. With `?from=<url>` it goes to that URL instead, but only for an `https:` URL on the host `zornscience.org` itself (no other host, port, user name or scheme; anything else keeps the default), exactly as in the Periodic Table and the Lewis and VSEPR Builder. Example: `?from=https%3A%2F%2Fzornscience.org%2Fscience%2Fchemistry%2Funit-2-quantum%2F`.
- The parameters combine, for example `?trend=ie&from=...&embed=1`.

## Completion code
A full mixed round (9 questions, all three trends) ends with a completion code under "Round complete", with a Copy code button, from `shared/zsa-codes.js` exactly as in the Ion Charge Trainer and the Ionic Formula Builder. Prefix `PTR`, activity id 4, for example `PTR-4TGG-8228-094M-T` (a 9-question round on 2026-10-16: 8, 8 and 9 of 9 on the first try, one Show me, two hints). The code holds the version, the day, the three first-try counts out of 9, and the Show me and Hint counts. A round of one trend (the picker or `?trend=`), one question (`?q=`) and Practice my misses give no code. Teachers read codes on `check/` ([TEACHERS.md](../TEACHERS.md)).

## Layout
- The same layout as the other activities: question strip and main-group table on the left, the step on the right from 900 px; strip, table, step on narrower screens. Both elements are marked in the table and read as "(in this question)".
- Measured fit (the step's buttons, feedback and hint, and the Next button on screen with the table in view, without the student scrolling): 360x640, 390x664, 1280x617 and 1366x657. The question strip is in view whenever a step starts. On a 360x640 phone, when feedback and a hint are both open, the engine scrolls so they stay in view and the question strip moves just above the screen while the table stays visible.

## Keyboard controls
The engine's: each answer group is one Tab stop; arrows, Home and End move without choosing; Enter or Space chooses and checks. Focus stays at the top on first load, moves to each new question's heading, to the next step's first choice, then to "Question complete" and "Round complete". Feedback is announced through a polite live region.

## Known limitations
- Main-group elements in periods 2 to 5 only, two elements at a time. No ionic radius, electron affinity or metallic character questions.
- Covalent radii are used throughout, so a few pairs that textbooks with other radius types would ask (O and F, for example) are excluded.
- Formative practice only: no saving or grade reporting. The completion code is a record of practice, not proof (see `shared/README.md`).
- Only Chromium was tested.
