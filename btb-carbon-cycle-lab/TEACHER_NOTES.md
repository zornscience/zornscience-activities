# BTB Carbon Cycle Lab: teacher notes

## Suggested use

1. Students make their hypothesis on the paper worksheet first.
2. Students open the lab and work through the five steps in one sitting (about 20 minutes).
3. They copy the Day 1 and Day 2 colors into the worksheet data table and write their variables and CER on paper.

## Key ideas to listen for

- "The foil tube turned yellow because the spinach kept respiring and CO2 made the water acidic."
- "The light tube turned blue because photosynthesis used more CO2 than respiration made. It did not stop respiring."
- "The tube with no spinach shows the color change came from the spinach."

## Design choices worth knowing

- The lab starts the BTB at green, not yellow. If the BTB starts yellow, the foil tube cannot get yellower, so the
  dark tube looks like it did nothing. The paper lab should start at green too. Students can overshoot to yellow or
  stay blue on purpose, which makes a good discussion.
- Wording is "net CO2 change". Plants respire in the light and in the dark.
- BTB tracks CO2 through acidity. It does not measure oxygen.
- Reading level is about grade 5 to 6, with short sentences.

## Calibrating to your real tubes

The biology rates are fitted for teaching. To tune them to your classroom: run one real green-start set, photograph the
tubes at 0, 4 and 24 hours, then compare with the lab's colors. Rates live in `model.js` (`params`).
