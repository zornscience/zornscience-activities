Find the harmonics

  1. Switch on the oscillator. Adjust the generator frequency until the string forms clear, large loops. Each resonance is a harmonic.
  2. Change the hanging mass, vibrating length, or string density. Explore how the resonance frequencies change. The harmonic buttons tune the generator exactly.
  3. Turn on Strobe lock to see the string at the same point in every cycle. This virtual synchronized light holds a stationary view without flashing the screen. Use the phase slider to inspect different positions.
  4. Show nodes and antinodes at resonance. Nodes stay still; antinodes have the largest motion.
  5. Move the two measurement cursors using their sliders, or drag their handles along the ruler. Adjacent nodes are half a wavelength apart: λ = 2Δx.

Normal viewing shows slow motion so you can follow the vibration. The generator displays the physical frequency. No observations are saved.

About this model

A lightly damped, small-amplitude string with ideal fixed ends and constant tension T = mg. The small driver is approximated as a node. A modal sum (16 modes, damping ratio 0.018) models the steady forced response; transients, pulley friction and string stretch are omitted. Away from resonance, several modes contribute and interior nodes are not stationary. Node markers appear only within 0.5% of a resonance. The string’s vertical displacement is magnified; horizontal distances and ruler readings use the selected vibrating length. The strobe samples one phase per driving cycle without visible flashes. Normal playback is 1/40 speed and may alias at very low screen refresh rates.
