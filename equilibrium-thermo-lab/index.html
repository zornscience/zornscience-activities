Investigate & understand the model
  1. At 298 K, add N₂O₄. Predict the shift before watching the graph.
  2. Compress the vessel. Why do both concentrations jump before the reaction shifts left?
  3. Heat the system. Explain why an endothermic forward reaction has a larger Kc.

Kc = [NO₂]²/[N₂O₄]. Qc uses the current concentrations. Qc < Kc favors products; Qc > Kc favors reactants. Equilibrium means equal forward and reverse rates, not equal concentrations.

Ideal-gas, constant-temperature relaxation model. Kc = 0.150 at 298 K; van ’t Hoff temperature dependence uses constant ΔH°. Simulated seconds are illustrative, not measured kinetics. Pressure uses P = nRT/V.

Investigate & understand the model

For metals: qwater = −qmetal, so cmetal = mwater × 4.184 × (Tf − Twater) / [mmetal × (Tmetal − Tf)]. Double the metal mass and compare ΔT.

For neutralization: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O. qsolution = m × 4.184 × ΔT; ΔH = −qsolution / mol H₂O formed. Try unequal reactant amounts to find the limiting reagent.

Ideal insulated calorimeter with negligible cup heat capacity, no evaporation, perfect mixing, and solution density 1.00 g/mL and specific heat 4.184 J/(g·°C). Neutralization uses −57.3 kJ/mol. Heat transfer is accelerated; the trace is illustrative.
