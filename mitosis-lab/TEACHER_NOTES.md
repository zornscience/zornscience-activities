# Mitosis Detective: teacher notes

## What students produce

The Copy step puts this on the clipboard, tab-separated, ready to paste into a Google Doc or Sheet:

- a table of cells per phase for fields 1 to 3, the total, the percent of cells and the estimated minutes (24 hour cycle)
- the total cells counted, the cycle length, the student's prediction, and the phases with the most and least time
- three conclusion sentence starters:
  1. Most of the cells we counted were in ______, so cells spend most of their time ______.
  2. The shortest phase was ______, because only ______% of the cells were in it.
  3. My prediction was ______. My evidence ______ my prediction because ______.

Written answers belong in your document; the lab has no typing boxes.

Without a seed every student gets different cells. Every sample is built so interphase is 80 to 90 percent of each
field, every phase appears in the total, anaphase is the shortest phase and prophase is the longest phase of mitosis.

## Check questions (step 5)

1. A pictured cell: anaphase.
2. Most cells are in interphase because it is the longest part of the cycle.
3. In anaphase, sister chromatids separate and move to opposite ends.
4. The meristem was sampled because its cells are dividing, so every phase can be found.
5. 5% of cells in prophase means a cell spends about 5% of its cycle in prophase (72 minutes of 24 hours).

## Simplifications to mention

- The cells are drawn, not photographed, with 6 chromosome pairs so each one stays readable (onion cells have 8 pairs).
- The 24 hour cycle is a typical round number; real onion root tip cycles vary with temperature.
- Percent of cells in a phase estimates percent of time only when cells divide at random times, which is close to true
  in a growing root tip.
- Cell plates are shown forming in telophase, as in plant cells; there is no pinching furrow.
