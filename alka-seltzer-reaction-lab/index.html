The Alka-Seltzer Reaction

Instructions

How does breaking up a tablet change the time it takes for the last visible piece to disappear?

  1. Make your prediction on your worksheet. Add 50 mL of water and 7 drops of BTB. Record the starting color.
  2. Use one whole tablet for the first trial. For the next trials, split one tablet into 4 pieces or crush one tablet between folded paper with the back of a metal spatula. Use all of the solid each time.
  3. Drop the tablet and start the timer. A virtual partner stirs gently at a steady speed. If you work in pairs, one of you can watch the beaker while the other stops the timer.
  4. Stop as soon as the last solid disappears. Bubbles may still rise. Record the time and final BTB color. If you stopped early, you can resume.
  5. Rinse and refill the beaker with fresh water and BTB before each trial. Use your readings for the graph and discussion on your worksheet.
Before you begin

The tablet contains sodium bicarbonate and citric acid. BTB is short for bromothymol blue, a liquid that changes color with pH.

In a physical lab

Wear safety goggles. Clean up spills right away. Tell your teacher if glass breaks.

After the investigation

Breaking a tablet exposes more surface to the water. You timed how long the solid stayed visible. You can use that time to compare your preparations.

When sodium bicarbonate and citric acid react, they form sodium citrate, water and carbon dioxide. The bubbles are escaping carbon dioxide gas. Some of the gas dissolves in the water. BTB shows a change in pH. Both dissolved carbon dioxide and other tablet ingredients can affect pH.

When carbon dioxide dissolves in seawater, it can lower the pH. This can leave less carbonate for animals that build shells. For the medicine question, think about why the time a product takes to dissolve matters to its designers.

About this activity
