# Teacher notes: Feed the Yeast

About 35 to 45 minutes. Works on Chromebooks and phones with no login. Nothing is saved: students should finish and
tap Copy my data before closing the tab, then paste into their document or worksheet (the data paste as tables).

## What students should find

- Part A: glucose and table sugar make big balloons (about 16 cm of growth), table sugar a little less because yeast
  must split it first. Starch grows about 2 cm, sweetener and no sugar about 1 cm (the yeast's own stored food).
- Part B: growth climbs from 0 to 10 g of glucose and then levels off; 20 g adds only about 1 cm. The marker is
  accepted from 8 to 15 g.
- Zoom: fermentation gives 2 ATP per glucose plus 2 carbon dioxide and 2 ethanol; respiration gives about 32 ATP.

Each visit varies a little (the plateau sits between 9 and 11 g, and readings have a little noise), so neighbors
will not have identical numbers. Use `?seed=42` on a projector to demonstrate exact numbers.

## Fair test discussion

Every student starts from a partner's draft with two mistakes chosen from: water at 20 or 50 °C, 2 or 8 g of yeast,
or 5 or 20 g of food. The checker names each mistake and the student fixes it. A good follow-up: why is the no-sugar
bottle still needed when it has no food? (It is the control.)

## Honest limits of the model

This is an illustrative teaching model, not measured yeast data. Real yeast saturate at far lower sugar
concentrations, ferment even with some oxygen present, and are affected by many things this lab holds constant.
Balloon circumference grows with the cube root of the gas inside, so the line graph bends a little more than the gas
volume does; where it levels off is the same.
