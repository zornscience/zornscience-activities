    Reference

    Equilibrium expressions

    aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD

    Kc = [C]c[D]d / ([A]a[B]b)

    Use products over reactants, raised to their balanced coefficients. Omit pure solids and pure liquids; retain dissolved species and gases. Kc uses equilibrium concentrations; Kp uses equilibrium gas partial pressures.

    All K and Q values are dimensionless here. Choose no units for them. Use M for concentrations and molar solubility. Concentrations approximate activities in ideal dilute solutions; gases are ideal.

    Q versus K

    Q uses the same expression as K with current values, not necessarily equilibrium values.

    • Q < K: shift right; net product formation.
    • Q > K: shift left; net reactant formation.
    • Q = K: dynamic equilibrium; equal forward and reverse rates.

    Changing the reaction equation

    Reverse: K′ = 1/K. Multiply coefficients by n: K′ = Kn. Add reactions: K = K1 × K2.

    Gas equilibria

    Kp = Kc(RT)Δn; R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K).

    Δn is the sum of gaseous product coefficients minus the sum of gaseous reactant coefficients. Use kelvin. At fixed temperature, concentration, volume, catalyst and inert-gas changes do not change K. Temperature can change K.

    A catalyst changes both reaction rates. An inert gas causes no shift at constant temperature and volume; at constant total pressure it can cause expansion and a shift when gaseous coefficients differ. Heating favors the endothermic direction.

    ICE tables and approximations

    ICE means initial, change, equilibrium. Use coefficient-weighted changes and substitute the equilibrium row into K. Discard roots that give negative concentrations.

    For a small-x approximation, check each neglected concentration change divided by its initial concentration, times 100, is under 5%. Otherwise solve the full equation. Keep extra digits and round only the final answer.

    Solubility equilibria

    MaXb(s) ⇌ aMb+(aq) + bXa−(aq); Ksp = [Mb+]a[Xa−]b.

    AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag+(aq) + Cl−(aq); Ksp = [Ag+][Cl−] = s2.

    CaF2(s) ⇌ Ca2+(aq) + 2F−(aq); Ksp = [Ca2+][F−]2 = 4s3.

    The s relations apply in pure water with no side reactions. For precipitation, use ion concentrations after mixing: Qsp > Ksp forms precipitate; Qsp = Ksp is saturated equilibrium; Qsp < Ksp is unsaturated.

    A common ion lowers molar solubility at fixed temperature without changing Ksp. Include the initial common-ion concentration in the ICE table. Ignore acid-base reactions and complex formation only when the prompt specifies no side reactions.
