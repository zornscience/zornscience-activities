Map the invisible

  1. Drag a positive or negative charge from the tray onto the paper, or press its Add button. Move charges to change the field.
  2. Drag the red probe tip across the grid. The voltmeter reads electric potential relative to infinity. Click the grid to move the probe there.
  3. Turn on equipotentials to reveal equal-voltage contours. Electric field arrows point in the direction a positive test charge would accelerate.
  4. Compare a single charge, a dipole and like charges. Select a charge to change its strength or remove it.

Keyboard: Tab to a charge or the probe, then use arrow keys to move it. Hold Shift for larger steps. Delete removes a selected charge.

This is an ideal point-charge sandbox, styled after a conductive-paper lab. It uses V = Σkq/r and E = Σkq r̂/r² in a plane through three-dimensional space. It does not solve a conductive sheet or fixed-voltage electrodes. Values within 1.2 cm of a charge are hidden; the point-charge model is singular there. Nothing is recorded.

About the model

Equipotentials are computed from the summed Coulomb potentials; field arrows show the negative gradient of potential. Red indicates positive potential, blue negative, and white zero. Arrows use a compressed length scale so weak fields remain visible. The voltmeter has an ideal reference at infinity. Physical thumbtacks on conductive paper instead set electrode voltages and produce a two-dimensional conduction problem.
